PharmAI

Drug interaction detection that knows Indian drug law
Built on Elasticsearch and AWS  ·  Live at medical.lehana.in/pharmai  ·  Demo video youtu.be/e1QXBwDv_ms  ·  Code github.com/Akum030/elastic_drugs

The blind spot every existing tool shares

Ask any drug interaction checker about Nimesulide and Paracetamol. It will tell you they are fine together. Both molecules are legal, and both sit on every pharmacy shelf in India. What no app will tell you is that their fixed-dose combination has been banned by CDSCO since 2016.

The ban lives inside a government gazette PDF, in a table that never uses the word "banned" in a single row. Google does not index it. We asked a leading AI model and it answered confidently, and wrongly. Meanwhile the stakes are real. A pharmacist who stocks the combination risks losing their licence under section 26A of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act. A patient who takes it risks liver injury. An insurer who pays a claim on a prohibited therapy has nothing to stand on when that claim is later contested.

India prohibits dozens of fixed-dose combinations every year. The information exists. It is simply unreachable at the moment of decision. That gap is our product.

What we built

PharmAI screens every drug pair through two lenses at once. The clinical lens asks whether the molecules interact. The regulatory lens asks whether this exact combination is legal to sell in India. A pair can pass the first and fail the second, and that is precisely the case every existing tool misses.

One rule anchors the whole design. The AI never answers from memory. Every verdict is reasoned only over what Elasticsearch retrieves from the indexed gazette corpus, and every answer must cite the exact notification it rests on.

Ask about Nimesulide and Paracetamol and the answer is concrete. Banned, notification S.O. 712(E), dated 10.03.2016, traceable back to the source row in the government document, typos and all. If the evidence is not in the corpus, the system says so plainly instead of guessing. For a drug safety tool, an honest "unknown" is a feature, not a failure.

It also meets users where they actually are. Type "Nimulid" and "Crocin 650" the way a real pharmacist would, and brands resolve to salts before anything is looked up. Hand it a FHIR bundle from a hospital record and it screens the entire medication list pairwise, because the dangerous pair is usually the one nobody thought to ask about. Speak to it in Hindi. Photograph a handwritten prescription and let it read the drugs off the page.

And every single verdict is written to an append-only, hash-chained audit ledger inside Elasticsearch before the user ever sees it. We tested that claim by deliberately tampering with a stored record. Verification caught the edit and named the exact entry. That property is what turns an AI answer into evidence a claims adjudicator or a regulator can actually rely on.

Architecture

Why Elasticsearch is the core

Four indices do four jobs. The gazette corpus is searched with hybrid retrieval, BM25 for exact salt names and notification numbers and vector kNN for the misspellings real users type, fused with reciprocal rank fusion over a custom pharma analyzer. The interaction knowledge base was partly mined out of the gazette tables themselves, 33 banned combinations each carrying its real notification number. Even the audit ledger is an Elasticsearch index with append-only writes and a SHA-256 hash chain.

Why AWS is the reasoning layer

Amazon Bedrock runs the thinking. Nova Lite works through the Converse API with tool use, so the agent chooses its own retrieval strategy against Elastic instead of following a fixed script. Titan Embed v2 generates the vectors, and the backend integrates Bedrock Knowledge Base with Amazon Kendra for gazette RAG. Sarvam AI is used narrowly for Hindi speech and prescription OCR, where no AWS equivalent exists.

Where this goes

Pharmacy chains need exactly this at the point of sale. Insurers need the audit trail for claims adjudication. And the approach generalises. Any country whose drug law lives in unstructured documents can be onboarded by pointing our ingestion agent at its regulator's archive.
